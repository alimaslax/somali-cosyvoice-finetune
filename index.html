Somali Voice Training

Abstract. This report presents the data and adaptation strategy used to specialize CosyVoice 3 for Somali speech synthesis. The training corpus was assembled from a fully audited collection of 8,745 Omar transcript records, with aligned audio evaluated for timing, speaking rate, signal compliance, and transcript integrity before inclusion. Fine-tuning updates the LLM and Flow modules so the model can learn Somali pronunciation, phrasing, prosody, and acoustic realization; the pretrained HiFT/HiFi-GAN vocoder remains frozen to preserve stable 24 kHz waveform generation. This separation concentrates learning capacity on language- and acoustics-specific adaptation while retaining a controlled synthesis backend for reproducible evaluation.

1. Generated audio

These samples are pure model generations from random Somali excerpts drawn from news and books. The excerpts are not part of the training set or validation set, and no reference audio was supplied or followed during synthesis. Each text is generated independently at the available slow, medium, and fast pace conditions, with a ChatGPT Voice generation included for comparison.

2. Corpus and pace

The processed corpus contains 66.30 hours across 8,742 clips from 170 recording folders. The median experienced pace is 137.7 words per minute; 66.29 hours have usable timed-word data. The pace analysis uses total clip duration, so it includes pauses and hesitation rather than measuring articulation alone.

The project source corpus is downloaded from the private Hugging Face dataset levenberg/omar-somali-asr. The local derived training build is deliberately separate: it packages reviewed audio and punctuation-aware text for CosyVoice 3 while preserving an audit trail back to those source records.

Speaking rate varies substantially across the largest recording folders. Corpus-level thresholds are useful descriptive references, but each clip is retained with its timing record so pace labels can be reviewed in context.

3. Data processing and cleaning

The source recordings are segmented with Silero VAD into coherent speech units, then denoised with DeepFilterNet 3 and loudness-normalised in a local batch workflow. Published clips are mono 24 kHz, 16-bit PCM FLAC: all 8,745 matched files meet this contract, and their JSON and FLAC durations differ by at most 0.0000 seconds. Temporary segmentation WAV files are not included in the published corpus.

Transcript processing preserves the source text and treats timing as evidence. Timed lexical words are compared with the canonical transcript before punctuation is proposed; mismatches are review flags. Inter-word gaps of 0.30 seconds propose commas and gaps of 0.75 seconds propose periods, producing 30,258 proposed marks across 7,111 auditable review records. The observed median gap is 0.04 seconds; no SSML tags, pause tokens, or invented Somali words are introduced.

The resulting CosyVoice 3 training dataset, Omar punctuation + pace instruction dataset V1, pairs each included clip with a truthful natural-language pace instruction. These instructions are training labels, not commands inferred at synthesis time: each one is assigned from the observed overall WPM for its source clip. The derived dataset retains the punctuation-V1 transcript and original FLAC audio while adding the instruction, numeric WPM, and pace tier as explicit fields.
